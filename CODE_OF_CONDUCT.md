# AetherCTR2 Code of Conduct

AetherCTR2 is built by a small team of volunteers, with partners outside the
project (Lynovation, who makes the CTR2-Max, and the SmartMTR author), for
radio amateurs around the world. We ask everyone involved to follow a few
ground rules. This isn't an exhaustive list; take it in the spirit intended.

This code applies to every space the project manages, including this
repository and its issues. It applies equally to the maintainer, the team and
the owners we support.

If you believe someone is violating it, email
[info@aethersdr.com](mailto:info@aethersdr.com).

- **Be friendly and patient.** Many CTR2-Max owners find firmware updates
  intimidating. Help them the way you'd want to be helped.
- **Be welcoming.** We welcome and support people of all backgrounds and
  identities, including but not limited to race, ethnicity, culture, national
  origin, color, immigration status, social and economic class, educational
  level, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, age, size,
  family status, political belief, religion, and mental and physical ability.
- **Be considerate.** Your work runs on other people's hardware and keys their
  transmitters. Weigh the consequences of your decisions for them, and for the
  partners whose work AetherCTR2 builds on. Remember that you may not be
  writing in someone's first language.
- **Be respectful.** Disagreement is no excuse for poor manners. Frustration
  happens; it must not become a personal attack.
- **Be careful in the words you choose.** Harassment and exclusionary behavior
  aren't acceptable. This includes, but is not limited to:
  - violent threats or language directed against another person;
  - discriminatory jokes and language;
  - posting sexually explicit or violent material;
  - posting, or threatening to post, other people's personal information;
  - personal insults, especially racist or sexist ones;
  - unwelcome sexual attention;
  - advocating or encouraging any of the above;
  - repeated harassment. If someone asks you to stop, stop.
- **When we disagree, try to understand why.** Disagreements happen. Resolve
  them constructively, focus on the problem rather than the person, and
  remember that it is human to err.

Adapted from AetherSDR's Code of Conduct, whose original text comes from the
[Speak Up! project](http://web.archive.org/web/20141109123859/http://speakup.io/coc.html).
